MedX — sources

Bibliography for MedX: what's deposited in this folder, what's left to consult online, and the neighborhood — work that does not describe this text-summarization module.

← MedX page

A tool published once, and only partially: a MICCAI 2025 workshop chapter describes the fine-tuning of the summarization model that MedX ships, but the module in the repository implements only a fraction of what the paper presents — the paper works within a multimodal framework (registered MRI and CBCT), MedX only reads text. The chapter is paywalled and not in PMC: nothing specific to MedX's own work could be retrieved here.

The lead is solid and verified on two points: the chapter's authors are indeed the repository's team (Gaydamour, Tulissi, Mattos, Miranda, Gurgel, Prieto, Cevidanes…), and the number of clinical indicators announced by the page — 56 — is exactly the number of keys in initialize_key_value_summary() (MedX_CLI/MedX_CLI_utils/dashboard_utils.py:343, recounted: 56). On the other hand the chapter's abstract is not freely distributed by Springer, Crossref, or OpenAlex: the figures reported in MedX.md (1,813 annotated segments, 500 TMD records, BART versus DeepSeek-R1) could not be re-checked from an open source and remain to be confirmed against the publisher's text.

Pitfall

The paper's key figures (1,813 segments, 500 TMD records, BART / DeepSeek-R1 comparison) come from a chapter abstract that is not freely distributed: they are reported in MedX.html but could not be re-checked against the publisher's full text.

Neighborhood

This work does not describe MedX and has no bearing on the module's automatic summarization code. It is included here to situate the clinical problem and the rest of the system that MedX was meant to be a building block of.

  • Caleme 2025 (file above): the imaging half of the CLIP 2025 paper, by a team that overlaps almost exactly with the chapter's — Gaydamour and Tulissi are co-authors on it. It covers the MRI/CBCT registration that the chapter describes as the multimodal side, and nothing about clinical text or summarization. It is the only article indexed in Europe PMC carrying Gaydamour's name.
  • Bianchi 2021 (file above): the same group's review of decision-support systems in temporomandibular joint osteoarthritis. It enumerates the clinical, imaging, and biological variables the group has been collecting for years — the vocabulary that MedX's 56 indicators turn into a schema. Four years older than MedX, with no mention of NLP.
  • Al Turkestani N, Li T, Bianchi J, Gurgel M, Prieto J, Shah H, Benavides E, Soki F, Mishina Y, Fontana M, Rao A, Zhu H, Cevidanes L. A comprehensive patient-specific prediction model for temporomandibular joint osteoarthritis progression. PNAS 2024;121(8):e2306132121, 10.1073/pnas.2306132121, PMC10895339, open access. The multi-source prognostic model that the CLIP chapter's patient/cohort dashboard is meant to feed into. Nothing about free text.

On information extraction from TMJ clinical records by this group: there is nothing else. A Europe PMC search for AUTH:"Cevidanes L" AND ("natural language processing" OR "clinical notes" OR "electronic health record" OR "large language model" OR summarization OR "text mining") turns up only a 2019 article unrelated to this (a statistical analysis of texture matrices). The CLIP 2025 chapter is the group's first and only NLP work.
