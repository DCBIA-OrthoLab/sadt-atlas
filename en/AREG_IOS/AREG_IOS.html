AREG_IOS

Longitudinal T1/T2 registration of Intra Oral Scans by ICP restricted to a stable region — a palatal patch predicted by a network (Butterfly) or a mucogingival band computed from predicted landmarks (MGL), processed in batches of folders.

Hutin 2023 · LNCS 14350 AREG_IOS conda env: shapeaxi Bibliography

Longitudinal T1/T2 registration of Intra Oral Scans. Same principle as FlexReg.md — a rigid ICP restricted to a stable region — but the region is predicted by a network instead of being drawn by the operator, and processing runs in batches of folders instead of patient by patient.

Two modes coexist in the same CLI:

  • Butterfly — upper arch, palatal patch predicted by a multi-view MonaiUNetHRes;
  • MGL — lower arch, geodesic band around the mucogingival line, built from the 13 MG landmarks predicted by ALI_IOS. It's the same code as FlexReg's MGL path, copied into the CLI.

1. Position in the chain

AREG_IOS is a CLI. Its only caller in the repository is the AREG module, via AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py. It doesn't call any other module itself: everything it needs (segmentation, orientation, landmarks) is produced upstream by the steps the orchestrator stacks ahead of it.

Semi_IOS / Butterflynothing: the scans must already carry Universal_ID and be orientedAREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:743

The Butterfly/MGL choice is a combo box on the widget (AREG/AREG.py:660), read by isMGLRegistration() (AREG/AREG.py:753) and passed as reg_type.

Conceptually upstream, FlexReg produces exactly the same input: an array of 0/1 points named Butterfly or Bottom_MGL. A scan patched by hand in FlexReg passes unchanged into AREG_IOS's ICP.

3. Inputs and preprocessing

3.1 T1/T2 pairing

Sort() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/dataset.py:98) and sort() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/dataset.py:267):

  1. glob over both folders, filtered to .vtk .vtp .stl .obj (the .tfm files are explicitly excluded);
  2. files are classified Upper/Lower by substring search: ["Lower","_L","L_","Mandibule","Md"] and ["Upper","_U","U_","Maxilla","Mx"];
  3. pairing works by literally stripping the T1 / T2 substring from the basename and comparing what's left;
  4. matching Upper↔Lower for the same patient works by additionally stripping the words Upper/Lower and comparing.
Pitfall

Non-negotiable assumptions: the name literally contains T1 or T2, and an arch marker. A _T4 pairs with nothing (the TIMEPOINT-SUFFIX comment in AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:318 points to the central note in AREG_CBCT/AREG_CBCT_utils/utils.py:83).

SortLower() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/dataset.py:173) is the MGL variant: a recursive glob (**/*), because CrownSegmentationcli writes into a subfolder named after the csv (liste_csv_file_T1_Seg), and it pairs lower arches alone, without requiring the matching upper pair.

3.2 Canonical orientation (Butterfly only)

DatasetPatch.__getitem__ (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/dataset.py:39) calls orientation() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/orientation.py:42) with the target [[-0.5,-0.5,0], [0,0,0], [0.5,-0.5,0]] and the teeth ["3","8","9","14"] = UR6, UR1, UL1, UL6.

It's the same algorithm as FlexReg.md §3 (two successive Rodrigues rotations + translation), but on different landmarks: FlexReg uses 3, 5, 12, 14 (UR6, UR4, UL4, UL6), AREG_IOS uses the molars and the central incisors, averaging their two centroids to get the midpoint. Same kind of result: occlusal plane ≈ z=0, +y anterior, +x patient's left side.

The frame is right-handed with x = patient's left and y = anterior, so z = x × y points inferiorly for a maxilla — the 7 cameras placed at z ≈ +0.9 therefore look at the arch from the occlusal side, the palate side. (A geometric deduction from the target, not a claim made by the code.)

Pitfall

A failed orientation doesn't stop the prediction: NoSegmentationSurf and ToothNoExist are caught, a warning is written, and the network runs on the scan as-is. The error message says "UR6, UR1, UL1 or UR6" — a typo, it should read UL6.

3.3 Normalization

ScaleSurf() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/transformation.py:181) recenters on the bounding box center and applies scale = 1 / ||bounds_max − center||. Then ComputeNormals (point normals, SplittingOff — mandatory, otherwise the order and count of vertices change).

The three tensors:

TensorContentShapedtype
Vvertices after orientation + normalization(N_v, 3)float32
Ftriangles, column 0 of GetPolys() removed(N_f, 3)int64
CNcolor-encoded normals: (n·0.5+0.5)·255 then /255(N_v, 3)float32

Despite its name, CN ("color normal") really is a color per vertex — GetColorArray (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/utils.py:83) loops over the points, not the cells. The round trip through 0–255 integers quantizes the normals to 8 bits; that's deliberate, training saw the same thing.

Important point

The network is fed the oriented/normalized surface, but the label array is written onto the raw surface (dataset.getUpperSurf(idx, time), read independently). Both share the same vertex order, so the indexing is valid, and the ICP runs in the input file's coordinates — not in the canonical frame.

4. The model — MonaiUNetHRes

Weights file: a single .ckpt in the model folder, selected by getModel(..., extension="ckpt") (AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:266). Source: AREG_model.zip, release HUTIN1/AREG v1.0.0 (AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:272). Loaded via torch.load(path)["state_dict"] — a PyTorch Lightning checkpoint.

4.1 Architecture

AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/net.py:61. A LightningModule that contains both the renderer and the network:

ElementValue
Backbonemonai.networks.nets.UNet, spatial_dims=2
Channelschannels=(16, 32, 64, 128, 256), strides=(2,2,2,2), num_res_units=2
Inputin_channels=4 — normals RGB + 1 depth channel

So it's an ordinary 2D UNet; everything "3D" about it lives in the rendering. The HRes in the name doesn't correspond to anything specific in the code.

The inspect.signature(DiceCELoss.__init__) block picks between weight and ce_weight depending on the installed MONAI version. class_weights is always None at inference, so this block's only purpose is to re-import the checkpoint without crashing.

4.2 The multi-view rendering

setup_ico_verts() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/net.py:114) — 7 camera positions; despite the name ico_verts, it isn't an icosphere:

[0, 0, 0.9]  [0.2, 0, 0.9]  [-0.2, 0, 0.9]  [0, 0.2, 0.9]
[0, -0.2, 0.9]  [-0.2, -0.2, 0.9]  [0.2, -0.2, 0.9]

Seven points clustered at z = 0.9, all looking at the origin (look_at_rotation, T = -Rᵀ·C). This is not a fly-by over the sphere like DentalModelSeg or ALI: it's a single occlusal view, slightly shifted seven times, which only makes sense because the scan was just placed into the canonical frame beforehand.

setup_render() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/net.py:132):

For each camera, render() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/net.py:184) produces:

  • images[..., 0:3] — the Phong rendering of the TexturesVertex(CN) texture, i.e. the normals;
  • zbuf — the rasterizer's depth map, concatenated as the 4th channel;
  • pix_to_face — for each pixel, the index of the face it hits, or -1 if the ray hits nothing.

Final shapes: X (1, 7, 4, 320, 320), PF (1, 7, 1, 320, 320), x (1, 7, 2, 320, 320).

PF is the pivot of the whole mechanism: it's the pixel → face link that lets a 2D segmentation be reprojected onto the mesh without ever doing inverse ray casting. The rasterizer is called twice (self.renderer(...) then self.renderer.rasterizer(...)) — the second call, without R/T, redoes the rasterization with the rasterizer's default camera. This works because MeshRenderer.__call__ has already pushed R/T into the camera's kwargs, but it's duplicated work on every view.

4.3 From the 2D prediction to the per-vertex label

AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/PredPatch.py:36:

x, X, PF = self.model((V, F, CN))
x = self.softmax(x * (PF >= 0))          # softmax dim=2, over the 2 channels
P_faces = torch.zeros(2, F.shape[1])
for pf, pred in zip(PF.squeeze(), x.squeeze(0)):   # loop over the 7 views
    P_faces[:, pf] += pred
P_faces = torch.argmax(P_faces, dim=0)
V_labels_prediction[F[0, :, 0]] = P_faces

Three things to understand:

  1. The background mask. x * (PF >= 0) zeroes out the logits of empty pixels. After softmax they become (0.5, 0.5) — not 0. So they contribute a half-vote to both classes, which cancels out at the argmax but dilutes the real votes. Faces of index -1 (the background) write into P_faces[:, -1], i.e. the mesh's last face, which ends up absorbing that noise.
  2. The accumulation doesn't really accumulate. P_faces[:, pf] += pred is an index_put_ without accumulate=True: when several pixels from the same view land on the same face (the general case, since a face covers several pixels), only one contribution survives, non-deterministically. The sum across the 7 views, though, does work (7 separate Python loop iterations).
  3. The face → vertex vote is degenerate. V_labels_prediction[F[0,:,0]] = P_faces only writes to the first vertex of each triangle. A vertex that is never in position 0 of its face list stays at 0. The patch therefore comes out full of holes by construction — and it's the morphological post-processing that repairs it, not a cosmetic refinement.
Confirmed bug

The three points above are facts about the code, not conjectures: the background mask dilutes the real votes without changing the argmax outcome, the index_put_ accumulation loses contributions without accumulate=True, and the vote only writes to F[:,0], leaving the patch full of holes by construction.

Then torch.where(>= 1, 1, 0), numpy_to_vtk, an array named Butterfly, added to the raw surface's PointData.

Confirmed bug

RemoveIslands(surf, arr, 33, 500, ignore_neg1=True) can't do anything: the targeted label, 33, never exists in the array (which only contains 0 and 1). It's dead code.

Dilation then erosion of the same radius = a morphological closing on the edge graph: the holes left by the first-vertex vote get filled in, and the boundary comes back to roughly where it was.

Connectivity is that of the incident cells (GetPointCells then GetCellPoints), so a 1-ring neighborhood. It's all pure Python, VTK call by VTK call, over ~100,000 vertices: it's the dominant compute cost after inference. NeighborLabel uses max(neighbor_labels, key=neighbor_labels.count), which is quadratic.

5. MGL mode — lower arch

The palate gives a stable plateau up top; the mandible has none. The substitute is the mucogingival line. This mode is the CLI port of path C of FlexReg (see FlexReg.md §5.3), and it involves no AREG network at all: the palatal .ckpt isn't even loaded (parameter_reg["model"] = "None", AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:92, and RunMGL returns before PredPatch).

5.1 The landmarks

MGLProcess() (AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:40) stacks two ALI_IOS calls (T1 then T2), unless the user has supplied a landmarks folder:

conda run -n shapeaxi python -m ALI_IOS \
  <input> <dir_models> None None \
  "LL6MG LL5MG LL4MG LL3MG LL2MG LL1MG L0MG LR1MG LR2MG LR3MG LR4MG LR5MG LR6MG" \
  <output_dir> 224 0 1 <log_path>

13 points, image_size=224, blur_radius=0, faces_per_pixel=1. Both timepoints write into the same temporary folder, and FindLandmarkFile() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:88) finds the json by the scan's stem: first <stem>_Lower_MG_Pred.json (ALI's convention, ALI_IOS/ALI_IOS.py:610), otherwise any json in the folder that contains the stem.

5.2 DropDoubtfulLandmarks

AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/mgl_patch.py:61. ALI writes into the markup's description field how it obtained a degraded point: "cameras aimed from an arch fit, tooth not segmented", "forced (confidence x.xxx)", "fallback (nothing predicted on the mesh)" (ALI_IOS/ALI_IOS.py:547). Any point carrying a non-empty description is dropped.

Verified

Code's justification (measured on 364 predictions, not published elsewhere): 4.2 mm median deviation from the neighbors' curve, versus 1.2 mm for the rest. Safeguard: if fewer than 3 points would survive, everything is kept.

OrderedMGLandmarks also accepts the legacy naming without the MG suffix (MGL_ORDER_LEGACY), and tolerates missing ones as long as 3 remain.

5.3 MGLPatch

AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/mgl_patch.py:227:

  1. SplineThroughLandmarks — an open vtkParametricSpline, SetUResolution(300) (DEFAULT_SAMPLES);
  2. SnapToSurface — each sample is snapped to the nearest vertex (vtkPointLocator), duplicates removed. An interpolated spline strays off the surface in interdental concavities;
  3. GrowBand — multi-source Dijkstra over the edge graph, weighted by the real Euclidean edge length, implemented with a binary heap (heapq), stopping at radius mm. Geodesic distance, not Euclidean: a buccal band can't leak through to the lingual side where the ridge is thinner than the radius;
  4. crowns are removed: LOWER_TOOTH_LABELS = range(18, 32) on Universal_ID, PredictedID, or UniversalID (whichever is found first). Without segmentation, a warning is issued and everything is kept;
  5. output array: Bottom_MGL (MGL_ARRAY_NAME), the same name as in FlexReg.

Special case radius == 0: no band, no spline, the patch reduces to the 13 snapped landmark vertices. This is a deliberate control case, meant to measure what the band adds over the points alone.

Differences from the FlexReg version (FlexReg.md §5.3):

In other words AREG_IOS has the simplified version: a symmetric band of constant height, with no fine-tuning. These are two separate files that diverged, not a shared module.

5.4 The MGL loop

RunMGL() (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:111): for each lower pair, patch on T1 and on T2, ICP, write both out. If no pair goes through, a RuntimeError is raised with the first error — a run that "succeeded" while leaving the output folder empty used to send users looking in the wrong place.

6. The ICP

Identical in both modes, only the array changes (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:191 and AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:204):

option = vtkMeshTeeth(list_teeth=[1], property="Butterfly")   # or MGL_ARRAY_NAME
icp = ICP([vtkICP()], option=option)
output_icp = icp.run(surf_T2, surf_T1)     # source = moving T2, target = fixed T1

vtkMeshTeeth.__call__ (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/vtkSegTeeth.py:87) doesn't build a mesh despite its name: it returns a vtkPolyData made up solely of isolated vertices, the ones whose array value is 1. list_teeth=[1] therefore doesn't mean tooth #1 but mask value 1.

The ICP on this point cloud (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/ICP.py:94):

icp.GetLandmarkTransform().SetModeToRigidBody()   # 6 dof, no scale
icp.SetMaximumNumberOfIterations(1000)
icp.StartByMatchingCentroidsOn()

Strictly the same block as FlexReg. No tooth enters the matching: those are precisely the structures that move between T1 and T2.

ICP.run returns a dictionary of which only matrix and source_Or (= ApplyTransform(source, matrix), i.e. the full transformed T2 surface) are consumed. As in FlexReg, vtkICP.__call__ returns source untransformed instead of the result: harmless here since list_icp only holds one method, but a multi-step chain would be wrong.

The composition (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/transformation.py:80):

final_matrix = np.linalg.inv(areg_matrix @ np.linalg.inv(matrix_aso))

matrix_aso is the orientation matrix written by PRE_ASO_IOS (ASO_IOS/PRE_ASO_IOS/PRE_ASO_IOS.py:422). The final inversion follows the ITK convention (fixed → moving). Pitfall

In Semi_IOS mode, the .tfm block isn't executed at all: the branch if args.areg_mode == "Auto_IOS" (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:315) is the only one. MGL mode produces no .tfm at all, regardless of areg_mode, since RunMGL returns before that point.

The log_path file receives a simple integer counter, read back by DisplayAREGIOS (AREG/AREG_Method/Progress.py:92), which divides by nb_patients × 3 for the progress bar.

11. Pitfalls and fragile points

Confirmed bug

isLowerUpper() tests the full path, not the basename (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS_utils/dataset.py:241; called with file as a full path from Sort and SortLower). A folder containing _U, L_, Md… misclassifies every arch. And the words are tested case-sensitively here, whereas Method.IsLower on the widget side (AREG/AREG_Method/IOS.py:141) lowercases the basename first: the two don't classify the same way.

Confirmed bug

The face→vertex vote only writes to F[:,0], and P_faces[:, pf] += pred doesn't accumulate pixels from the same face. These are facts about the code, not conjectures; the patch gets repaired afterward by the morphological closing.

Confirmed bug

RemoveIslands(..., 33, 500, ...) can't do anything: dead code.

Confirmed bug

check_platform() looks for 'Microsoft' with a capital letter (AREG_IOS/AREG_IOS.py:45). WSL2 reports itself as microsoft-standard-WSL2, lowercase, so the WSL branch is probably never taken, and it's the else branch (package imports) that runs instead. Just as well: the WSL branch mixes from AREG_IOS_utils.transformation import ... with from AREG_IOS.AREG_IOS_utils.transformation import ..., which requires both the repository root and AREG_IOS/ to be on the PYTHONPATH.

Pitfall

T1's .tfm often can't be found. AREG_IOS looks for <patient_id_short>_SegOr.tfm where patient_id_short = name_t2.split("_T2")[0].split("_")[0], while PRE_ASO_IOS named it after PatientNumber() = basename.split('_U')[0].split('_L')[0] (ASO_IOS/ASO_IOS_utils/utils.py:295). The two only agree if the arch marker comes before the timepoint (P001_Upper_T1.vtk ✔, P001_T1_Upper.vtk ✘). The failure is a warning, not an error.

Confirmed bug

WriteSurf always uses vtkPolyDataWriter but keeps the input extension: an input .stl comes out as a .stl file containing legacy VTK.

Confirmed bug

os.mkdir (not makedirs) in WriteSurf: a nested output folder that doesn't exist yet makes the write fail.

Confirmed bug

DatasetPatch.isLower() tests is not None, not the length. If Sort returns an empty lower list, lower evaluates to True and every access raises an IndexError, caught as a warning.

Confirmed bug

sort() and insideLower() use continue where break was expected: sort() can pair the same T1 with several T2 files if the names collapse together.

Pitfall

A failed orientation stops nothing. An unsegmented scan still goes to the network, in some arbitrary frame, with 7 cameras looking from the wrong side. The patch will be silently wrong.

Pitfall

AREG_IOS.xml is out of sync with the CLI (index 4 missing, 4 MGL parameters absent). Harmless as long as launching goes through conda, but any attempt to go back through slicer.cli.run will break.

Pitfall

patch_radius is clamped to [0, 20] mm on the widget side (AREG/AREG.py:734), whereas FlexReg stops at 5 mm. Beyond the height of attached gingiva, the band spills over onto mobile tissue.

12. Literature

The published foundation is AReg IOS: Automatic Registration on IntraOral Scans (Hutin, Anchling, Cevidanes et al., Lecture Notes in Computer Science, 2023, MICCAI): crown segmentation by DentalModelSeg, initial alignment via shared centroids, palatal ROI segmented by multi-view 3D shape analysis, then ICP.

Two discrepancies between the paper and this repository's code:

  • the paper describes an initial alignment by shared crown centroids; in the code, that role is played by upstream PRE_ASO_IOS plus VTK's StartByMatchingCentroidsOn() — there is no centroid-alignment step inside AREG_IOS itself;
  • the MGL path doesn't exist in the paper. It's a later internal development, unpublished to date, and the figures cited in its code (4.2 mm vs 1.2 mm over 364 predictions) aren't sourced anywhere else than in the comments.
