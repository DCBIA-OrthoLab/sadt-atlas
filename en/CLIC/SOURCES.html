CLIC — sources

No publication describes CLIC: the only primary source is a NA-MIC Project Week 43 project page, the rest is bibliographic neighborhood on a related problem.

7 files retrieved ← CLIC

No publication describes CLIC. Checked: nothing under the module's name, nothing under its authors' names, no article, no preprint, no IADR/AADOCR abstract found. The only primary source is a NA-MIC Project Week 43 project page (Montreal, 2025), retrieved into this folder (2025_Tulissi_NAMIC_PW43_impacted_canines.md), which presents CLIC as an already-existing tool and describes ongoing work that extends it to root-resorption severity. Its "Background and References" section is empty ("No response"): the authors themselves attach no reference to it.

What is documented elsewhere is the problem, not this code. CLIC's specificity — a bicortical class in addition to the buccal/palatal pair, predicted slice by slice in 2D on CBCT by a Mask R-CNN — does not appear in any of the neighboring work below. Do not present their numbers as belonging to the module.

  • No NA-MIC Project Week page devoted to CLIC outside of PW43. Scanning the full NA-MIC/ProjectWeek repository tree (PW33 through PW45) returns only the one page on impacted canines cited above. The PW43 "Universal Tooth Labeling Module" page (same authors) covers a different subject.
  • The code's original repository, ashmoy/maskRcnn (created January 2025), cites no publication: no description, and its README.md is a copy of SlicerAutomatedDentalTools's.

Neighborhood

This work does not describe CLIC's code.

  • Buccal/palatal localization on panoramic radiographs. Kahraman 2026 (BMC Medical Imaging) and Helal 2026 (Children) train 2D image-classification CNNs on panoramic radiographs, using CBCT as ground truth. Only two classes, one image per patient. CLIC works directly on the CBCT volume, slice by slice, with three classes.
  • 3D segmentation of impacted canines on CBCT. Swaity 2024 (Scientific Reports) and Ünal 2025 (BMC Oral Health) segment the impacted canine with a volumetric network (nnU-Net / 3D CNN). They delineate the tooth; they do not classify its cortical position.
  • Root resorption of adjacent teeth. Pirayesh 2024 (BMC Oral Health) proposes a hierarchical approach to diagnosing resorption induced by an impacted canine on CBCT. This is exactly the extension announced by the PW43 project above — so the closest neighbor to the ongoing work, but not to the code currently in the repository: CLIC, as checked in, predicts no resorption severity.
  • The architecture. He 2017 describes Mask R-CNN. The module uses the torchvision.models.detection.maskrcnn_resnet50_fpn implementation; neither the article nor the torchvision documentation says anything about canines.
