AREG_CBCT — register a follow-up CBCT onto the baseline

AREG_CBCT superimposes two CBCT scans of the same patient taken at two different times (T1 and T2), anchored on a bony region that's assumed not to have changed — the cranial base, the mandible, or the maxilla. The registration itself is a classic geometric computation, no neural network involved: reproducible, but entirely dependent on picking a good anchor.

When to use it

Whenever you need to compare two CBCT scans of the same patient — before/after treatment, growth follow-up, post-surgical check — and they need to share the same spatial frame to be overlaid or measured. AREG_CBCT only does this voxel-to-voxel registration on a stable bony structure: your two scans must already belong to the same patient and represent two distinct time points.

Three modes, one registration

All three modes end up computing the same registration; they only differ in what happens beforehand.

  • Semi-Automated — you supply the reference-region masks on T1 yourself. The most direct route if that work is already done.
  • Fully-Automated — AREG_CBCT computes those masks for you. Only reliable when your T1 scans are already oriented consistently with each other.
  • Orientation & Registration — AREG_CBCT starts by centering T1, placing landmarks on it and orienting it to a common reference, before computing masks and registering. The most complete mode, for T1 scans with no consistent orientation to begin with.
Which one to pick

Already have masks? Semi-Automated. No masks, but T1 scans already oriented the same way? Fully-Automated. Neither? Orientation & Registration. In all three modes, you check one or more reference regions (Cranial Base, Mandible, Maxilla): each one runs its own independent registration, with its own output.

What it needs, what you get

What to provide

  • A baseline CBCT (T1) and a follow-up CBCT (T2) per patient, named with the _T1 and _T2 suffixes — that naming is what lets them be paired automatically, nothing else
  • Accepted formats: .nii.gz, .nii, .nrrd, .nrrd.gz, .gipl, .gipl.gz
  • In Semi-Automated: a folder of reference-region masks on T1, already prepared
  • In Fully-Automated and Orientation & Registration: the segmentation and orientation models, downloaded once
  • One or more reference regions checked

What comes out

  • One folder per registered region, holding for each patient the registered T2 CBCT and its matching transformation matrix (.tfm)
  • The T1 CBCT and your original files are left untouched
  • If "Merge Segmentations" is checked, the final segmentations of T1 and of the registered T2

Steps

  1. Pick a mode. Semi-Automated, Fully-Automated, or Orientation & Registration — see above.
  2. Point it to the T1 and T2 folders. Check that each pair of files shares the same patient id, followed by _T1 or _T2.
  3. In Semi-Automated, set the T1 masks folder. Without it, there's nothing to register against.
  4. Check the reference region(s). Cranial Base, Mandible, Maxilla — each one triggers its own registration.
  5. Download the models the first time you run it, then, if you want, check under "Review and manual adjustment" the steps you'd like to inspect before the run continues.
  6. Set an output folder and launch. If review pauses are enabled, the run stops after each checked step: you inspect the result, nudge it with the mouse if needed — for the registration, by dragging the overlaid scan — then move on to the next patient or resume the full run.

How long it takes

The registration itself runs on CPU, no graphics card needed, and on a single thread to stay reproducible from one run to the next — a choice that favors stability of the result over speed. The steps around it (automatic mask segmentation, orientation) are much faster with a GPU. The paper describing the method reports under five minutes per patient for the whole chain; without a GPU for the upstream steps, budget more time on a large series.

If something's off

A patient goes missing, with no error message

T1/T2 pairing only works with the _T1 and _T2 suffixes in the file name. Any other convention (_T3, _baseline, a date…) keeps AREG_CBCT from recognizing the pair, and that patient is silently dropped — nothing is shown to flag it. If a patient is missing from the output, the first thing to check is that patient's file names.

Avoid mask keywords in a scan's file name

AREG_CBCT recognizes a mask file by keywords in its name (seg, mask, pred, or the region's abbreviation — cb, mand, max). The first file that matches is used as-is, with no check that it's actually a mask. A scan named, say, P1_T1_MAX.nii.gz could therefore be mistaken for a maxilla mask.

DICOM import

Convert your CBCT scans to .nii.gz before running AREG_CBCT. DICOM input is only offered in Orientation & Registration mode, and stays fragile even there: starting from already-converted files avoids the surprise.

The saved matrix can drift slightly from the best alignment found

The .tfm file and the registered CBCT stay consistent with each other, but both can drift a bit from the alignment the registration actually found as optimal. The gap depends on where the skull sits in the volume, and stays small when your images are already well centered — which is the case after a run through Orientation & Registration. If you reuse this matrix elsewhere, in AutoMatrix for instance, always check the overlay by eye before trusting it.

"Include Approximation Step" no longer does anything

The checkbox stays checked by default in the interface, but it has no effect on the result: don't count on it to speed up or refine a registration that's giving you trouble.

What's next

The registered CBCT and its matrix are directly reusable — in AutoMatrix, in the chain's analysis tools, or for a simple visual overlay in Slicer. As with any automatic registration, a quick visual check before using it for a measurement remains good practice. Also note that the matrix doesn't account for any centering applied upstream to T2: to bring the follow-up scan all the way back to its original frame, you need to chain that step together with the AREG_CBCT matrix, not use one without the other.

Further reading

On AREG_CBCT itself

  • Anchling et al., LNCS 14242, 2023 — Automated Orientation and Registration of Cone-Beam Computed Tomography Scans. The module's paper: orientation then voxel-based registration on a region of reference, with the error measured on a patient series. free full text.

Around the method

  • Ruellas et al., PLOS ONE 2016 — 3D Mandibular Superimposition: Comparison of Regions of Reference for Voxel-Based Registration. Which mandibular region to take as a reference, and why the choice changes the result. This is the decision you make in the interface.
  • Ruellas et al., AJODO 2016 — Comparison and reproducibility of 2 regions of reference for maxillary regional registration with cone-beam computed tomography. The same question on the maxillary side.
  • Cevidanes et al., Dentomaxillofac Radiol 2005 — Superimposition of 3D cone-beam CT models of orthognathic surgery patients. The lab's founding reference: the cranial-base superimposition, done by hand, that AREG_CBCT automates.

The tool's full bibliography — what was found, and what was looked for without success — is in its sources.
