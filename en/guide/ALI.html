ALI — place landmarks automatically

ALI drops anatomical landmarks onto your scans for you. It works just as well on a CBCT (a volume) as on an intraoral scan (a surface), using two different engines behind a single window.

On video
  • ALICBCT 3 min 49 · DCBIA Videos channel
  • ALIIOS 2 min 52 · DCBIA Videos channel

When to use it

Any time you need landmarks and placing them by hand would take too long: a dozen points on one patient is manageable; on a series of forty, it isn't anymore. ALI also supplies the landmarks for several other modules — if you run an automatic orientation or registration, it may already be running behind the scenes without you having to open it.

Two engines, one window

The module picks the right engine on its own based on your data: CBCT for a volume, IOS for an intraoral scan. All you do is say what type your files are and which points you want.

What it needs, what you get

What to provide

  • A folder of scans — CBCTs as .nii.gz, or intraoral scans as .vtk
  • The group of points you want, checked in the list
  • The trained models, downloaded once

What comes out

  • One landmark file per scan and per group, in Slicer's format (.mrk.json)
  • Opens straight into the scene, points draggable with the mouse
  • Your original scans are left untouched

Steps

  1. Download the models. The "Download latest models" button fetches the trained weights. Only needed once, but essential: without them the module has nothing to predict from.
  2. Choose the data type. CBCT or intraoral scan. This decides which engine gets used.
  3. Point it to the input folder. ALI processes the whole folder in one go; there's no need to run it patient by patient.
  4. Check the points you want. They're grouped by region. Only check what you actually need: every point adds computing time.
  5. Set an output folder, then launch. A progress bar tracks the patients as they go through.

How long it takes

Expect around 15 seconds per point per patient with a graphics card, and up to a minute without one. Over ten points and thirty patients, that gap stops being trivial: roughly an hour versus five. The module runs fine without a GPU, but plan for an overnight run on a large series.

If something's off

"Nothing comes out, or the points look wrong"

First check that the models were actually downloaded, then that the data type you checked matches your files. A CBCT processed as an intraoral scan won't produce anything usable.

Intraoral scans: teeth need to be identified first

On an intraoral scan, the engine aims its measurements tooth by tooth. If your file doesn't have crown identification yet, ALI runs it automatically along the way — expect extra time on the first attempt.

DICOM import

Use .nii.gz files instead. The module's DICOM path doesn't work in automatic mode.

Disk space

The temporary folder isn't cleared afterward. On long series, remember to clean it out from time to time.

What's next

The landmarks you get are a starting point, not ground truth: open them in Slicer and check them before using them for a measurement or a registration. They can be dragged with the mouse like any other markup.

Further reading

On ALI itself

  • Gillot et al., Orthod Craniofac Res 2023 — Automatic landmark identification in cone-beam computed tomography. The paper behind the CBCT path: agents that walk through the volume to the landmark. Reports the measured error, landmark by landmark.
  • Baquero et al., CLIP 2022 (LNCS 13746) — Automatic Landmark Identification on IntraOralScans. The paper behind the mesh path: the surface is photographed from several viewpoints, and each pixel votes for a landmark.

Around the method

  • Alansary et al., Medical Image Analysis 2019 — Evaluating reinforcement learning agents for anatomical landmark detection. Where the walking-agent idea comes from, and what it costs compared with regressing the coordinates directly.
  • Boubolo et al., SPIE Medical Imaging 2021 — FlyBy CNN: a 3D surface segmentation framework. The multi-view rendering the mesh path is built on.

The tool's full bibliography — what was found, and what was looked for without success — is in its sources.
