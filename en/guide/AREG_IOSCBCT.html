AREG_IOSCBCT — place an intraoral scan into a CBCT's frame

AREG_IOSCBCT registers an intraoral scan (IOS) onto the CBCT of the same patient, taken at the same visit. It has nothing to do with AREG_IOS, which compares two scans from two different visits: here the two inputs come from different sensors — optical and volumetric — that have to be brought into agreement.

On video
  • Automated registration of intra oral scan with CBCT using an accurate and quick workflow 45 s (overview) · DCBIA Videos channel

When to use it

Whenever you want the surface detail of an intraoral scan — crowns, contact points — and the bone anatomy of a CBCT in the same frame: for a visual check, a combined measurement, or simply to overlay both images from one visit. The module does the alignment; there is nothing to draw or register by hand.

Three ways to get there

Depending on what you already have — nothing, raw scans, or landmarks already placed — the module offers three modes that don't ask for the same input files. See "Three modes" below before you start.

What you need, what you get

To provide

  • Intraoral scans in .vtk only — no other format is read
  • CBCT in .nii.gz only — same rule
  • File names carrying the patient id, a timepoint (T0, T1 or T2), and the arch (U for upper, L for lower) as standalone tokens, not buried inside another word
  • Depending on the mode: landmarks already computed, or nothing beyond the raw scans

What comes out

  • One registered scan per arch and per patient, in .vtk
  • One landmark file per arch, in Slicer's format (.mrk.json), carrying the registered scan's coordinates
  • Normalized output names — they may no longer look exactly like your input names

Three modes, which one to pick

Manual

  • You already provide the occlusal landmarks on both sides: an IOS folder, a CBCT folder, an IOS landmark folder, a CBCT landmark folder
  • The module predicts nothing, it registers directly
  • Pick this if another module (ALI) already placed your points

Semi-automated

  • You provide the raw IOS and CBCT, no landmarks
  • The module segments the scan's crowns, then predicts landmarks on both sides before registering
  • Pick this if your CBCT is already oriented but you have no landmarks yet

Fully automated

  • You provide only the raw scans, CBCT and IOS
  • The module chains resampling, orientation, segmentation and landmarks before registration — seven steps ahead of the registration itself
  • Pick this if you're starting from nothing; it is also the slowest mode

Steps

  1. Pick the mode that matches what you already have — see the section above.
  2. Check your file names before launching anything. A file named P001_T1_U_IOS.vtk is recognized; a timepoint outside T0–T2, or an arch letter glued to the rest of the name, is not.
  3. Point the module to the folders the chosen mode asks for — IOS, CBCT, and where relevant the landmark folders.
  4. Set an output folder, then launch.
  5. Open the log when it's done if the number of registered patients is lower than expected — that is where skipped patients are reported.

If something's wrong

No matrix is written

Registration produces surfaces that are already transformed, not a matrix file. Unlike AREG_IOS or AREG_CBCT, you cannot reopen the result and nudge it with the mouse: if the alignment is off, the fix is to rerun — with different landmarks or a different setting — not to correct it by hand.

One scanned arch means the patient is dropped

Every patient must provide both arches, scan and landmarks included. If even one is missing — say, only one arch was ever scanned — the patient disappears from the result with no visible error, only a line in the log.

Only T0, T1 and T2 are recognized

The clinical timepoint is read from the file name, and only those three values count. A file marked T3 or beyond matches nothing: the patient is simply left out.

The CBCT surface threshold is fixed

The surface extracted from the CBCT for registration always uses the same threshold value, whatever your scanner or reconstruction. On a CBCT whose units aren't calibrated, the resulting surface may not correspond to bone or teeth at all, and the registration then runs against noise.

The progress bar can announce more patients than it will deliver

The interface's counter also recognizes .nrrd, .nii and .gipl for the CBCT. The engine itself only accepts .nii.gz: a patient counted at the start may never actually be processed.

What happens next

The registered scans open directly in Slicer, next to the original CBCT. Since no matrix is kept, the only check available is visual — inspect the overlay before using it for a measurement. Also check the log for any line saying the pre-alignment was skipped: that would mean a batch came out without the first registration step, and nothing else would have told you.

Further reading

No publication describes this module's multimodal path. The AReg IOS paper covers registering one intra-oral scan onto another over time, not matching a mesh with a CBCT.

Around the method

  • Zheng et al., Clinical Oral Investigations 2025 — Automatic multimodal registration of cone-beam computed tomography and intraoral scans: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The state of the art on the problem this module tackles, with the errors reported by published methods. The best entry point.
  • Kim et al., Bioengineering 2023 — Novel Procedure for Automatic Registration between Cone-Beam Computed Tomography and Intraoral Scan Data Supported with 3D Segmentation. A neighbouring approach, open access: segment the teeth, then match. Useful to compare with what the module does.
  • Jang et al., Medical Image Analysis 2024 — Fully automatic integration of dental CBCT images and full-arch intraoral impressions with stitching error correction via individual tooth segmentation and identification. Goes further: also corrects the intra-oral scan's stitching error, the defect you will most often see on a full arch.

The tool's full bibliography — what was found, and what was looked for without success — is in its sources.
