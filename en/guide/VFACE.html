VFACE — classify facial asymmetry

VFACE measures and classifies skeletal facial asymmetry from a single CBCT per patient. You don't need a second scan for comparison: the module builds its own "mirror" of the patient and compares the patient against that reflection.

On video
  • Classify facial Asymmetry and longitudinal studies, here is VFACE the new tool who do both 33 s (overview) · DCBIA Videos channel

When to use it

Whenever you want to objectify a facial asymmetry on a CBCT — quantify it rather than eyeball it — and possibly find out whether it involves the mandible, the maxilla, or both. VFACE starts from a raw volume: no segmentation or landmarks to prepare beforehand, it handles all of that itself.

The heaviest module in the extension

VFACE is not a standalone tool: it's an orchestrator that chains, one after another, six other modules of the extension (resampling, orientation, bone segmentation, registration, dental surface segmentation, classification). On a batch of patients, expect the full run to be long — on the order of an hour per patient depending on your machine — and it keeps the other tools busy while it runs. Plan to launch it when you don't need the other modules at the same time, typically overnight on a series.

What you need, what you get

To provide

  • A folder of raw T1 CBCTs, one per patient (.nii, .nii.gz, .nrrd, .nrrd.gz, .gipl, .gipl.gz)
  • No prior segmentation or landmarks: the module starts from the volume alone
  • The trained models (ALI, AMASSS, DentalSegmentator, the VFACE classifiers), to download once
  • An output folder, on a drive with room to spare

What comes out

  • A per-patient table of measurements (skeletal distances and angles)
  • A final classification file stating whether the patient is symmetric or asymmetric, and whether the asymmetry involves the mandible and/or the maxilla
  • Optionally, bone surfaces and color distance maps (heatmaps) to visualize where the discrepancy sits

Steps

  1. Download the models. A dedicated button fetches all the weights needed by the chained modules. Only needed once.
  2. Point to the input folder containing your T1 CBCTs, and to an output folder.
  3. Choose the analysis mode. For an asymmetry study (the common case), VFACE builds the patient's mirror and produces the classification. A "longitudinal studies" mode exists to compare two real time points of the same patient, but it doesn't produce a classification in that case.
  4. Choose what you want as output. Measurements and classification (quantitative mode), surfaces and distance maps (visualization mode), or both.
  5. Launch. The pipeline processes patients one after another. Review pauses are inserted at key steps: they load the intermediate results into the scene and let you check them, even correct landmarks or adjust a registration, before continuing.

How to read the result

The final deliverable is a classification table, one row per patient, with three pieces of information:

  • Symmetric / Asymmetric — the overall verdict.
  • Mandible: yes / no — evaluated only if the patient is classified as asymmetric.
  • Maxilla: yes / no — evaluated under the same condition, independently of the mandible.

These last two are not mutually exclusive: a patient can be flagged asymmetric on both the mandible and the maxilla. The detailed measurement table (distances and angles, alongside the classification) stays available if you want to see what a given patient's verdict is based on.

Further reading

On VFACE itself

  • Buisson, poster IADR 2026 — Automated Classification of Facial Asymmetry, Identification of Regional Asymmetry Patterns. The poster presented at the 104th IADR General Session, San Diego. The UNC Adams School of Dentistry round-up gives its title and author; the poster itself is not deposited anywhere public, and the IADR archives are not freely indexed.
  • SPIE Medical Imaging 2026 — Automated classification of skeletal facial asymmetry in CBCT using a reproducible 3D Slicer workflow. The only publication describing the pipeline. Paywalled, and to be read knowing it describes a classification method different from the one the module actually ships.

Around the method

  • AlHadidi & Cevidanes, Dentomaxillofac Radiol 2011 — Comparison of two methods for quantitative assessment of mandibular asymmetry using cone beam computed tomography image volumes. Compares two ways of measuring mandibular asymmetry: mirroring on the mid-sagittal plane, or mirroring then registering on the cranial base. This is the approach VFACE automates.
  • Cevidanes et al., Oral Surg Oral Med Oral Pathol 2011 — Three-dimensional quantification of mandibular asymmetry through cone-beam computerized tomography. Quantifies asymmetry after mirroring and registration, and spells out what the measured gaps mean clinically.
  • Evangelista et al., Angle Orthodontist 2022 — Prevalence of mandibular asymmetry in different skeletal sagittal patterns: A systematic review. Prevalence of mandibular asymmetry across sagittal skeletal patterns — to place a patient against a population.
  • Peng et al., European Journal of Orthodontics 2026 — Automated assessment of 3D facial asymmetry: a systematic review. The systematic review of the problem: the existing automated methods and how they perform. The best recent entry point.

The tool's full bibliography — what was found, and what was looked for without success — is in its sources.
