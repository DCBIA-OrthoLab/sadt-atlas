User guide

What each tool does, what to feed it, what it returns — without going into the code. For the internal mechanisms, switch to the Atlas.

Who this guide is for

Anyone who opens SlicerAutomatedDentalTools in 3D Slicer and wants to use it: clinicians, researchers, students. It assumes no ability to read code and no idea what a neural network is. If what you want is the inner workings — which model is called, when, on what data — you want the Atlas instead: both sections cover the same 20 modules, each from the opposite end.

Why it exists

The extension gathers 20 modules written by different teams at different times. Their documentation amounts, depending on the case, to a few lines of README, a project page, or nothing at all. Several behave differently from what their publication describes: the paper documents a version the code no longer runs.

This guide was written by reading each module's code, not its documentation. So it says what the tool actually does when you click, including when that is inconvenient: 79 warnings and 4 bugs are flagged at the exact point where you are likely to hit them. Nothing is fixed here — the point is that you do not lose a day to it.

What every page contains

They all follow the same plan, so you know where to look without rereading:

  • When to use it — which problem the tool answers, and the cases where it is of no help.
  • What you need, what you get — the expected inputs, their format, and what comes out at the end.
  • Step by step — the steps in order, field by field.
  • When it goes wrong — the common failures and what causes them.
  • Further reading — the publications behind the tool where there are any, and the pointer to the Atlas page.

Where to start

If you know which tool you are after, take it from the list below. Otherwise start from what you have to do:

  • Compare two scans of the same patient over time → Registration
  • Isolate structures in a CBCT → Segmentation
  • Place anatomical landmarks, or put a scan back upright → Landmarks & orientation
  • Measure, classify or predict from a shape → Analysis
  • Crop or apply a matrix, in batch → Utilities
  • Extract or anonymise clinical text → Text & language
