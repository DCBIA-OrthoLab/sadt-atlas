ALI — sources

ALI's bibliography: what has been archived in this folder, what's left to read online, and the neighborhood — related work that doesn't describe the code.

← ALI sheet

ALI is the best-published module in the repository: each of the two backends has its own paper, and those papers describe the code well. ALI_CBCT is covered by an open-access journal article (Orthod Craniofac Res 2023), ALI_IOS by a paywalled LNCS chapter (CLIP 2022) with an author's version in circulation. The sheet's reservation holds up: the MG path (mucogingival line) of the ALI_IOS code is not described in any publication — no article, no proceedings, no findable IADR/AADOCR poster (see "What was searched for the MG path").

  • ALI_CBCT's .xml is the full Europe PMC JATS (fullTextXML); the .txt is the readable version, bibliographic header included.
  • FlyBy CNN, Alansary: Europe PMC exposes no JATS for these (NIH author manuscripts). The full text was retrieved through the NLM's BioC API (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/bionlp/RESTful/pmcoa.cgi/BioC_xml/<PMCID>/unicode). So the .xml files here are BioC, not JATS.
  • The CLIP PDF is the author's version: deposited on ResearchGate on March 11, 2024 by Najla Al Turkestani (co-author), then mirrored on the personal site of Juan Fernando Aristizábal (also a co-author). The publisher's version remains paywalled at Springer.
  • No PDF could be pulled from Wiley, PMC, or Europe PMC: all three hosts return a Cloudflare page, not a file.

What was searched for the MG path

Verified

The sheet claims the mucogingival line is published nowhere. Verified, and nothing contradicts it:

  • Europe PMC: "mucogingival" AND AUTH:"Cevidanes L" (13 results, none about automatic landmark identification), "mucogingival" AND AUTH:"Prieto JC" (1 result, unrelated), "mucogingival" AND "intraoral scan" AND "deep learning" (3 results, all unrelated abstract collections).
  • OpenAlex: ALIIOS, mucogingival line automatic landmark identification, gingival margin landmark detection intraoral scan neural network — nothing from the DCBIA group.
  • The CLIP paper itself does not contain a single occurrence of the word mucogingival: it only knows occlusal and cervical landmarks ("CB" cervical buccal, "CL" cervical lingual), over 5 spherical views.
  • Bianchi's AAOF report, which lists the project's publications and abstracts through 2023, also contains no occurrence of mucogingival.
  • IADR/AADOCR abstracts: the archives sit behind an IADR account. The group's three abstracts identified by the AAOF report (ALIDDM 2022, ALI_CBCT 2022, IOS segmentation+landmarks 2023) are listed above; none of their titles concerns the mucogingival line. Targeted searches within the iadr.abstractarchives.com domain surface nothing else for Cevidanes/Prieto.
  • The code itself cites no reference for the MG path: the only figures available (155 training scans, 4–21 mm, 364 predictions) live in the code comments.

The published work closest to the MG path comes from an unrelated team (Brandenburg et al. 2022, Freiburg im Breisgau, archived here) and reconstructs the mucogingival line on optical scans of plaster models, without deep learning. On the DCBIA side, the only publication that uses the mucogingival junction is Ioshida et al. 2019, which validates it as a registration reference for mandibular digital models — that clinical use is what justifies ALI_IOS predicting these points, but it is not a description of the MG model.

Neighborhood

Related work that does not describe ALI but sheds light on the problem.

  • Alansary et al. 2019 (archived here): a comparative evaluation of reinforcement-learning agents for anatomical landmark detection. This is the general reference for the moving-agent mechanism described in the sheet.
  • Ghesu et al. 2016 (MICCAI) and 2019 (TPAMI): the origin of this mechanism, an artificial agent followed by multi-scale deep reinforcement learning. This is reference 20 of the ALI_CBCT paper, the only one it cites for the agent part. Maxime Gillot has no prior work of his own on the subject: a review of his publications (OpenAlex, Europe PMC) turns up, before ALI_CBCT, only AMASSS (skull segmentation, PLOS ONE 2022) and a narrative review on implant planning. ALI_CBCT is his first paper on reinforcement-learning agents; the prior art belongs to Ghesu, not to him.
  • FlyBy CNN 2021 and DentalModelSeg 2023 (Prieto is senior author on both; first authors Boumbolo and Leclercq): multi-view crown segmentation. The CLIP paper explicitly says it uses this as a preprocessing step to segment and number the teeth, and the ALI_IOS code still calls dentalmodelseg. So these two papers describe an ALI_IOS dependency, not ALI_IOS itself.
  • Woodsend B, et al. 2022, Development of intra-oral automated landmark recognition (ALR) for dental and occlusal outcome measurements, European Journal of Orthodontics 44(1), 43-50 — 10.1093/ejo/cjab012, PMCID PMC8789266. A competing method, used as a point of comparison in the CLIP paper's discussion (0.389 mm against 0.43 mm for ALIIOS).
  • Wu TH, et al. 2021, Two-stage mesh deep learning for automated tooth segmentation and landmark localization on 3D intraoral scans, arXiv:2109.11941. The other point of comparison in the CLIP paper (iMeshSegNet + PointNet-Reg, 0.597 ± 0.761 mm).
  • Gillot M, Baquero B, Le C, et al. 2022, Automatic multi-anatomical skull structure segmentation of cone-beam computed tomography scans using 3D UNETR, PLOS ONE 17(10), e0275033 — 10.1371/journal.pone.0275033, PMCID PMC9555672, open access. This is the AMASSS paper, not ALI's, but it shares authors, the CBCT dataset, and the Slicer infrastructure.
