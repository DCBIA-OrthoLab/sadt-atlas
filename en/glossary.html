Reference frames and geometry

direction
3×3 matrix giving the orientation of the voxel grid's axes in physical space. Together with the origin and the spacing, it forms the index-to-millimetre transform.
isosurface
Surface connecting every point of a volume that shares the same intensity value. AREG_IOSCBCT extracts one at threshold 400 to give its ICP a geometric target.
LPS
Left–Posterior–Superior: the three axes point toward the patient's left, the back, and up. This is the frame used by DICOM, ITK, and this repository's .vtk files. ALI_CBCT and ALI_IOS both write "coordinateSystem": "LPS" in their JSON.
mesh
A surface described by vertices and triangular faces, as opposed to a volume of voxels. IOS scans are meshes; CBCT scans are volumes.
marching cubes
The algorithm that builds an isosurface: it walks the grid cube by cube and emits the triangles that cross the requested threshold in each one. Output: a mesh, not a volume.
origin
Physical position of the voxel at index (0,0,0). It's the only one of the three metadata fields that a crop changes without resampling.
RAS
Right–Anterior–Superior: Slicer's convention, opposite to LPS on the first two axes. Converting between the two just multiplies x and y by −1. Nearly every sign bug in this repository traces back to an LPS↔RAS conversion that was forgotten, or done twice.
spacing
Physical size of a voxel, in millimetres, along each of the three axes. An "isotropic 0.3" volume has spacing [0.3, 0.3, 0.3]. Resampling changes the spacing; cropping doesn't.
voxel
The three-dimensional pixel: one cell of the volume's grid. Its content is an intensity, in Hounsfield units if the CBCT is calibrated.

Registration

affine
12 degrees of freedom: rigid, plus scale and shear. Straight lines stay straight and parallel; distances don't.
elastix
Image-registration library driven by parameter files. AREG_CBCT and MRI2CBCT call it through itk-elastix for their voxel-to-voxel registration.
ICP
Iterative Closest Point: for each moving point, find its nearest neighbour in the target, estimate the rigid transform that brings them closer, and repeat. Converges well only if the starting point is already close — hence the systematic landmark pre-alignment.
Kabsch
The algorithm that solves Procrustes: recenter both point clouds on their centroid, decompose the covariance matrix by singular value decomposition, and reassemble the rotation. A determinant correction rules out reflections.
landmark
An identifiable anatomical point, placed by hand or predicted by a network. Used either as an orientation reference or as a correspondence for pre-alignment. Stored as .mrk.json.
mrk.json
Slicer's point-list format (markups). Carries the coordinates, their labels, and the coordinateSystem that says whether it's LPS or RAS.
Procrustes
The problem of optimally aligning two paired point sets: which rotation and translation minimize the sum of squared differences. Unlike ICP, the correspondences are known in advance.
registration
Finding the transform that brings a "moving" image or surface into the frame of a "fixed" one. Longitudinal when both come from the same patient at two dates, multimodal when they come from different sensors.
rigid
A transform with 6 degrees of freedom: three rotations, three translations. It preserves distances — so no scale, no shear, no deformation.
SVD
Singular value decomposition. Here, the computational tool behind Kabsch, and behind estimating a local frame from a point cloud.
tfm
Extension of an ITK transform file. Holds a matrix, not an image. When a module doesn't write one, the operator can't nudge its result by hand with the mouse — which is the case for AREG_IOSCBCT.

Networks and learning

reinforcement-learning agents
ALI_CBCT doesn't segment: it moves agents through the volume step by step, each one learning to get closer to its landmark. The search stops when an agent starts oscillating, then resumes at a finer resolution.
checkpoint
A file of trained weights. Reading its hyperparameters is often the only reliable way to know what a model actually does — several code/paper discrepancies in this repository were established that way.
cross-encoder
A model that takes the query and the document together and outputs a relevance score, unlike embeddings, which encode them separately and then compare. More accurate, but impossible to precompute.
distillation
Training a small model to reproduce the behaviour of a large one. MiniLM-L6, used by Agent, is distilled from a 12-layer model.
EfficientNet
Family of convolutional image networks that jointly scales depth, width, and resolution. DOCShapeAXI's multi-view branch.
fly-by
Technique of rendering a 3D mesh from several viewpoints, running a 2D network's prediction on each view, then reprojecting the predictions onto the surface. It avoids having to convolve in 3D.
Grad-CAM
An explainability method: gradients of the predicted class weight a layer's activation maps, producing a map of what mattered. In DOCShapeAXI it only covers the multi-view branch.
heatmap
A map predicted by a network whose maximum gives the location being searched for. This is how ALI_IOS localizes a landmark on a rendered view.
LightGBM
Gradient-boosted decision tree library. VFACE embeds three of them, binary and supervised, where its paper describes unsupervised clustering.
MONAI
PyTorch library specialized in medical imaging: transforms, architectures, sliding-window inference.
nnU-Net
Segmentation framework that picks its own architecture, preprocessing, and hyperparameters from the data. This is what AMASSS runs today, even though its paper describes a UNETR.
UNETR
Segmentation architecture with a transformer encoder and a convolutional decoder. Described in AMASSS's paper, but replaced in the code by nnU-Net.

Text and language models

BART
Pretrained encoder–decoder model for text generation. MedX uses a large-cnn variant fine-tuned for summarization.
GGUF
llama.cpp's weight format, designed for CPU inference and quantisation. This is the format both Ollama and CNE require.
llama.cpp
C++ inference engine for language models, optimised for CPU. Runs under Ollama, and directly under CNE.
Ollama
Local server that downloads, caches, and serves language models over an HTTP API on port 11434. Decides GPU usage on its own — hence the trap with the snap install.
Presidio
Microsoft library for detecting and replacing personal data in text. Combines rules with named-entity recognition.
quantisation
Lowering weight precision — from 16 bits down to 4, typically — to shrink size and speed up inference, at the cost of some quality. Q4_K_M names one such scheme.
spaCy
Natural-language-processing library. Supplies Presidio with entity recognition — people, places, dates.

Anatomy and modalities

arch
The full set of teeth on one jaw. Upper and lower are treated as two separate inputs by nearly every module.
CBCT
Cone Beam Computed Tomography: a cone-beam scanner, producing a 3D volume. Low dose, high spatial resolution, but intensities are often not calibrated in Hounsfield units — which makes any hardcoded threshold fragile.
condyle
The head of the mandible, articulating in the temporal fossa. A central structure in asymmetry analyses.
FOV
Field Of View: the physical extent covered by the acquisition. A "small FOV" captures only one region, which changes which models apply.
IOS
Intra Oral Scan: an optical impression of the arches, produced by a mouth scanner. It's a surface, never a volume.
occlusal
Relating to the contact surface between the upper and lower teeth. The O suffix on landmarks (UR6O) denotes the tooth's occlusal point.
TMJ
Temporomandibular Joint. Target of MRI2CBCT and part of MedX.
Universal_ID
Label array placed on an IOS mesh by dentalmodelseg: each vertex is assigned the number of the tooth it belongs to, following universal numbering.
