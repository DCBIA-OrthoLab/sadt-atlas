AREG_IOSCBCT with VISOR

What changes when you run AREG_IOSCBCT through a VISOR server from 3D Slicer: what to provide, what is the same, what is different. The legacy module has its own page.

VISOR Legacy page Atlas (VISOR)

AREG_IOSCBCT places an intraoral scan in the frame of a CBCT of the same patient, taken at the same visit. With VISOR the registration is computed on a server: in Slicer you fill a form, send the folders and get the results back. The registration itself is pure geometry and needs no graphics card, but the two modes that predict the landmarks use tools that do. This page was written from the VISOR code (SADT-VISOR 6ba2a78, VISOR-serve e9a7e5d, AutomatedDentalToolsRemote 19b95e8); the details are on the Atlas page.

What changes in the panel

  • Open AREG Remote (category “Automated Dental Tools Remote”) and pick IOS to CBCT in the first field, Mode.
  • Automation has three choices. Registration (the default here; the same name, but the last choice, in the legacy module) takes your two landmark folders and calls no other tool. Semi-Automated labels the crowns and predicts both landmark sets. Fully-Automated first orients the CBCT, then does the same.
  • You send Intraoral scans and CBCT volumes as two folders, or the hosted test cohorts, plus, in Registration mode, the Intraoral landmarks and CBCT landmarks (.json or .mrk.json).
  • The models and the orientation reference are chosen by the server and their fields are hidden.

What you provide

  • Meshes in .vtk or .stl, and CBCTs in any of the usual volume formats, not only .nii.gz.
  • Each mesh names its arch as a separate word of its name (U, Upper, L or Lower), and so does each intraoral landmark file. Both arches of a patient are no longer required: a mesh without landmarks fails alone and the others go on.
  • The patient is matched between the two folders by the digits of its name. See the warning below.

What stays the same

The same two stages: a rigid fit on the landmarks both sides share, then an ICP against the CBCT isosurface. Each arch is registered on its own, so the occlusion between the two arches is not constrained. The level of the isosurface is read off the scan, as it already was in the legacy module at the commit this site describes.

What is different in the results

  • Each registered mesh comes with its composed 4x4 matrix as a numpy file (_matrix.npy). Still no .tfm, and the registered landmarks are no longer saved.
  • The output is one folder per patient key (the digits of the identifier), holding files named after each mesh, with AREG_report.json at the root. For each mesh it says whether the landmark fit was accepted and why not, and how well the ICP matched. You no longer need to read the log for a skipped pre-alignment.
  • A mesh whose ICP matches under 5% of the arch is refused rather than written.
  • The level of the isosurface can no longer be overridden.
Patient names that differ only by letters are merged

The two modalities are paired on the digits of the patient identifier, leading zeros removed. A12 and B12 are the same patient for the tool, and so are P001_T1 and P001_T2. Observed by running the VISOR pairing code: with the meshes of both time points of one patient and several CBCTs for the same digits, a single patient was formed, only the first CBCT in sort order was used and the others vanished without a word, and a T2 mesh was given the landmarks of the T1. Send one patient, one time point and one CBCT per run, and give each patient a number of its own.
