AREG_IOSCBCT on VISOR

AREG_IOSCBCT places an intraoral scan of the teeth into the frame of the same patient’s CBCT. The two scans come from different machines and share nothing but the teeth, so the tool matches landmarks first, then refines on the surface. This page explains how, step by step, with the maths. Where the code does something worth checking, a link leads to the code page. The legacy module is described on its own page.

VISOR Code (developers) AREG_IOSCBCT reached as AREG, mode IOS to CBCT no torch, pure geometry Legacy page Guide (VISOR) Bibliography

Overview: what goes in, what comes out

A CBCT is a 3D volume of grey values; an intraoral scan is a surface of triangles with colour or tooth numbers. Nothing in them looks alike, so AREG_IOSCBCT works in two stages: a quick rough placement from 12 matching landmarks, then a fine slide of the mesh onto the surface of the teeth extracted from the CBCT. The tool itself contains no network: the landmarks are given or asked from other tools.

What it is
Inputintraoral meshes (.vtk or .stl, one file per arch) and CBCT volumes, paired by a patient number in their names; in Registration mode, a landmark file for each mesh and each CBCT
Idea12 occlusal points exist on both scans; a rigid fit on those pairs brings the mesh near, then an ICP slides its crowns onto the CBCT surface
Outputeach mesh moved into the CBCT frame, its 4×4 matrix, and AREG_report.json saying what each mesh went through

Three modes decide how much the tool does for you: Registration (the default) takes your landmark files and calls nothing; Semi-Automated labels the crowns and predicts the landmarks; Fully-Automated also orients the CBCT first.

1. Position in the chain

A client sends AREG with the mode “IOS to CBCT” and the server swaps the general AREG form for this engine. The engine has no network of its own: tooth labels, landmarks and orientation are asked of other tools, and what is left is geometry.

In the codeThe facade, the declared calls, the resources and the state of the tests

2. Files involved

The engine is a small Python package: one file reads and writes meshes and runs the per-patient loop, one pairs the files, one turns the CBCT into a surface, one holds the geometry (landmark fit and ICP), and one asks the other tools. Nothing needs a GPU.

3. What runs where, and the pipeline

Slicer only builds the form and unpacks the result. Everything else runs on the server: the calls to the other tools, the surface, the fits, the files.

In the codeWhat the server and the client each do, step by step

3.1 The supervised calls

In the two automatic modes, the tool first asks for what you did not give. ASO orients the CBCT onto a Frankfurt reference (Fully-Automated only). Crown_Seg labels the teeth of the meshes. ALI_IOS places the occlusal landmarks on the meshes, and ALI_CBCT places twelve named occlusal landmarks on the CBCT. ALI_CBCT is asked for those twelve points by name, because asking by region would run every agent of every region that contains one of them. The meshes are labelled but not oriented: the landmark fit alone brings them near.

In the codeThe four calls, in order, with their arguments and progress marks

3.2 Pairing the two modalities

The mesh and the CBCT of a patient are named by different conventions, so the tool keeps only what they share: the digits. The time-point tokens t0, t1, t2 are removed, then every non-digit, then the leading zeros: P001_T2_U.vtk and P_0001_T2.nii.gz both become patient 1. A landmark file is attached to a mesh by the jaw in its name (u upper, l lower); one file without a jaw, which is what ALI_CBCT writes for a whole CBCT, serves both arches because the labels themselves tell them apart (UR1O against LR1O). The crudeness of that key is the first known issue.

In the codeThe key function, the folder walk and the landmark-file matching

3.3 The CBCT surface, and the level it is contoured at

Once per patient, the volume becomes a surface: all voxels brighter than a level L are separated from the others by a closed skin of triangles (the marching-cubes contour). That skin takes bone and enamel alike, with no distinction. The level is read off each scan, because a CBCT is not calibrated and a fixed number can be meaningless.

On the published test scan the engine reads an enamel plateau of 2783, a surround of 146 and a level of 1464, far above the fallback of 400; one landmark, UL3O, reads at most 274 around it, under the level, so it is judged not to lie on a tooth. The same level judges each landmark: a point whose neighbourhood never reaches it is not on enamel, which in a closed bite usually means the network put it on the opposing arch.

In the codeThe probe radius, the soft-tissue band, the fallback and the contour call

3.4 The landmark fit

At least 3 common labels are needed. A landmark judged off enamel is set aside before any fit, as long as 3 pairs remain. Trimming then removes one pair per round and never goes below 4 pairs. If the final residual is above 10 mm the pre-alignment is refused (the mesh then starts from where it was); if the landmarks are nearly in a line, the tool only warns. A refused pre-alignment is not an error: the ICP goes on from the pose the orientation left.

In the codeThe fit, its constants, the table of rules and the line numbers

3.5 The ICP

The ICP refines what the landmarks started. The part of the CBCT surface kept as a target is a box around the arch’s landmarks plus 25 mm; the points of the mesh that steer the fit are the crowns (the gingiva has no counterpart in a CBCT), when the mesh carries tooth labels and at least 10% of it is crown. The whole arch, gingiva included, is then moved by the transform they find.

The ICP stops when the largest shift stays under a thousandth of a millimetre for 3 rounds, or after 300. An ICP that matched under 5% of the arch is an error for that mesh, so an unregistered mesh is never written under a registered name. After the ICP, the tool compares the landmarks before and after: more than 1 mm of drift is reported as a warning. The final matrix is the ICP composed after the landmark fit.

In the codeThe seven rules of the ICP, the fallback step and the report fields

3.6 Outputs and the report

For each patient, a folder named by the digit key holds the registered meshes (.vtk, with their tooth labels kept) and, for each, its composed 4×4 matrix as a numpy file. There is no .tfm and no re-saved landmark file. AREG_report.json lists, per mesh, the landmarks used and dropped, whether the pre-alignment was accepted and its residual, and the ICP statistics. A volume that cannot be contoured does not stop the run: the meshes are then placed on their landmarks only and the report says the ICP did not run.

4. What you can choose

  • The folders: one of intraoral scans, one of CBCT volumes; every scan format is read, recursively.
  • The mode: Registration (you supply two landmark files), Semi-Automated or Fully-Automated.
  • The landmark files, in Registration mode only, one set for the meshes and one for the CBCTs.
  • The models, only in the two other modes; each tool called finds its own weights when none is named.
  • The suffix of the output names (Reg by default).

Nothing sets the contour level or the ICP distance in the panel: they are read off the scan and fixed at 1 mm.

5. What is predicted vs computed

Everything inside the engine is computed. The landmarks and the labels are predicted by other tools, or supplied by you.

BlockNature
CBCT orientation, crown labels, landmarkspredicted by other tools (ASO, Crown_Seg, ALI), unless you send the landmarks
Surface level and contourcomputed medians of the scan, marching cubes
Landmark fitcomputed closed-form rigid fit, trimming
ICPcomputed point-to-plane, with a KD-tree (a structure that finds the nearest point quickly), on the CPU

6. Environment

The engine runs on the CPU in its own Python environment, with pyvista, SimpleITK, numpy, scipy and vtk; it imports no torch and no GPU library. Its nearest-neighbour queries use every core. It declares no time limit.

In the codeThe pinned versions, the data the server holds and the timeouts

7. What changed from the legacy logic

The rules are the same: same level, same fit, same ICP. What changed is the wrapping.

  • Where it runs: a server engine behind the AREG form, not a Slicer command-line module.
  • Pairing: by the digits of the names, recursively, and with every scan format; the legacy matched letters plus digits and a time point, in flat folders, and skipped a T3.
  • Arches: each mesh is registered alone; the legacy demanded both arches of a patient.
  • Pre-alignment: the verdict of the fit goes to the report, instead of a log line.
  • Outputs: the mesh and a matrix per mesh; no re-saved landmark files.
  • The contour level can no longer be overridden.
  • The orientation of the CBCT is one call to ASO; the intraoral meshes are not oriented at all.

8. Known issues in VISOR

  • Patients whose names differ only by letters are merged into one, and a second CBCT of the same number can disappear without a word (details).
  • The contour level can no longer be set by hand, although a warning still advises it (details).
  • On the published test scan the level is 1464, not 400, so any figure drawn at 400 shows more bone than the ICP sees (details).
  • The documentation of max_dist says 1.5 and the code uses 1.0 (details).
  • A comment in the code describes an upstream function that no longer matches the legacy (details).

Two things work well: the verdict on each pre-alignment is data in the report, and the scratch folder is always removed.
