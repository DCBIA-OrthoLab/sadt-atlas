VFACE

VFACE decides whether a face is skeletally asymmetric from a CBCT scan alone. It learns and segments nothing itself: it chains six other modules to register the scan onto its own mirror image, measures the gap, and classifies the asymmetry with three LightGBM models. This page explains how, step by step, with the maths. Files, lines and pitfalls are on the code page.

SPIE Medical Imaging 2026 Code (developers) vface_cli conda env: none (Slicer's Python) Bibliography VISOR

Automatic classification of skeletal facial asymmetry on CBCT. VFACE learns nothing and segments nothing itself: it is an orchestrator that chains six other modules from the repository, extracts linear and angular measurements from them, and finishes with three LightGBM classifiers. It is the highest-level module in the chain: the call tree is the central piece of information here.

The clinical principle fits in one sentence: T2 is the patient’s own mirror image. There is no second scan. The scan is oriented, mirrored across the mid-sagittal plane, registered onto the original, and everything that gets measured is a gap between the patient and their own reflection.

This page describes the legacy Slicer module (SlicerAutomatedDentalTools at 3c67957, 2026-10-26). The VISOR port, which runs the same tool on a server, is on its VISOR page; it exists on feature branches only and has never been run with its six real callees.

Overview: what goes in, what comes out

VFACE
Inputa folder of CBCT scans (T1), one per patient; no segmentation and no landmark is needed
Ideaorient each scan, mirror it, register the mirror back region by region, measure the gaps between landmarks and their mirrored counterparts, and let three small models read those measurements
OutputExcel tables of measurements and features, a classification per patient (asymmetric or not, mandible, maxilla), optional heat maps on bone surfaces

1. Where it sits in the pipeline

Nothing in the repository calls VFACE: it is a terminal module. It does, however, call eight processes, seven of which are other modules of the extension. Everything is planned in one pass, which produces a list of steps; the panel then runs them one at a time.

CalledRole in VFACE
MRI2CBCT resample0.3 mm isotropic resampling and recentring
ASO, centring stepgeometric centring, writes a transform
ALI-CBCTlandmarks (orientation, then measurements)
ASO, orientation steporientation onto a gold-standard case
AMASSScranial base, mandible and maxilla bone masks
AutoMatrixapplies the mirror and the registration matrices
AREG-CBCTrigid Elastix voxel registration
The VFACE classifierLightGBM classification

Three steps are plain Python functions run on Slicer’s main thread rather than separate commands: the measurements, the bone surfaces (nnU-Net) and the heat maps, plus the landmark derivation and the post-processing.

The call tree, in “Full pipeline” + “Asymmetry Assessment” + “Quantitative + Visualization” mode

T1: one raw CBCT, nothing else

Preparation

  1. resample to 0.3 mm isotropic, centred
  2. PRE_ASO ×2, for the maxilla and the cranial base
  3. ALI places 6 maxillary landmarks, then SEMI_ASO orients on occlusal + mid-sagittal
  4. ALI places 7 cranial-base landmarks, then SEMI_ASO orients on Frankfort + mid-sagittal
  5. AMASSS segments CBMASK and MANDMASK on the CB scan, MAXMASK on the MAX scan

Mirroring

  1. AutoMatrix applies Matrix_mirror.tfm to the oriented scans: this is where the “T2” comes from

Registration

  1. AREG ×3, once per region: cranial base, maxilla, mandible

Measurements

  1. ALI places every measurement landmark, on the CB scan (the scans are first padded by 30 mm so landmarks at the edge stay reachable)
  2. derive_landmarks carries them CB → MAX by a rigid fit
  3. AutoMatrix mirrors the landmarks
  4. AutoMatrix applies the three AREG matrices to them
  5. AQ3DC ×3 measures distances and angles
  6. post-processing assembles the feature row
  7. VFACE_CLI runs the three LightGBM models

Visualization

  1. BatchDentalSeg ×5 builds the bone surfaces
  2. ModelToModel Distance ×3 writes the asymmetry maps

2. Files involved

The module is a 3,664-line panel (interface, review pauses, batch navigation, sequential execution, cleanup), a 2,722-line file that is the pipeline plan together with the landmark derivation, the post-processing and the heat maps, a vendored copy of the AQ3DC measurement module, the segmentation logic, a 946-line file holding the measurement formulas, the review catalogue, a worker for the heat maps and the 293-line classifier command. There is no conda environment: everything runs in Slicer’s own Python, and only joblib and lightgbm are installed on top. The full table is on the code page.

In the codeThe file table with line counts, the dependency check and the CUDA setting

3. Inputs and preprocessing

Input: a folder of T1 CBCTs, one per patient, in a common volume format. No segmentation, no landmark: VFACE starts from the raw volume. An empty folder is refused. The patient identity is read from the file name by cutting it at known decoration words; only the T1 and T2 timepoints are recognised, so a third timepoint would silently break the pairing.

Resampling and centring.

The centring step is purely geometric: the volume’s centre is translated to the origin and the translation is saved in a transform file. No network is involved.

Orientation: two distinct frames, built on the same centred scan.

FrameLandmarks requested from ALIGold standard
Maxilla (MAX)ANS, IF, PNS, UL6O, UR1O, UR6OOcclusal and mid-sagittal plane
Cranial base (CB)Ba, LPo, N, RPo, S, LOr, ROrFrankfurt horizontal and mid-sagittal plane

The orientation does a three-landmark initialisation then a rigid ICP of the patient’s landmarks onto the gold standard’s, and writes the inverse of (orientation after centring) as a transform. After that, the mid-sagittal plane sits at x = 0.

The mirror. The mirror matrix shipped with the AutoMatrix release is a pure reflection along the x axis in LPS, with no translation: symmetry about the mid-sagittal plane. AutoMatrix applies it to volumes by resampling and to landmark files by transforming their points.

Coordinate system: everything is in LPS. ALI writes its landmarks in LPS, so does the landmark derivation, and the measurements read positions without conversion; the signs of the measurement components follow from this. The only RAS to LPS flips are in the manual review and in the VTK exports of the segmentation.

In the codeInput formats, the patient-identity cuts, the resample parameters, the mirror matrix and the coordinate flips

4. The models called

VFACE contains only one model of its own, the LightGBM classifiers. The others belong to the upstream modules, downloaded when the panel opens.

ModelNature
ALI-CBCTreinforcement-learning agents, a DenseNet
AMASSS3D UNet, label map output
DentalSegmentatornnU-Net 3d_fullres
Symmetry, mandible and maxilla modelsLightGBM, saved as joblib files

4.1 ALI-CBCT

network One agent per landmark moves inside a 64-voxel neighbourhood, at two scales (1 mm then 0.3 mm), with six possible moves. The landmarks requested for the measurements are the deduplicated union of the points named in the mandible and cranial-base lists, so a landmark shared by both does not trigger two agents. How an agent walks is explained on the ALI page.

In the codeThe agent settings, the output group files and the deduplication line

4.2 AMASSS: the masks

network Two calls: the cranial-base and mandible masks on the cranial-base-oriented scan, the maxilla mask on the maxillary one. These masks are only used to mask the fixed image of the registration, not to measure anything.

In the codeThe call settings and the names of the mask files

4.3 BDS / DentalSegmentator: the surfaces

network nnU-Net produces a 5-class label map (upper skull, mandible, upper and lower teeth, mandibular canal) that is converted into surfaces by marching cubes, smoothing and decimation, then written in LPS. These surfaces serve only the heat maps. The model weights (about 220 MB) are downloaded on first use, and three other models offered by the code are never selected by VFACE.

In the codeThe marching cubes chain with its parameters, the weight check and the unused models

4.4 The registration: AREG-CBCT

computed Elastix, no learning. The original scan is masked with the corresponding AMASSS mask, then a rigid registration is run in three resolutions with a mutual-information metric, one thread for determinism. It is done once per region.

Outputs: for each region and patient, the registered scan and its transform. The mask is chosen by a keyword in its file name and the first match wins, which is fragile (details).

In the codeThe Elastix parameters, the output names and the keyword-based mask selection

5. The algorithmic core: the measurements

5.1 The measurement types (seven rows, ten exact strings)

All are built from the type of measurement column of the Excel list, matched by exact text. Positions are in LPS.

TypeInputsWhat it computes
Distance between 2 points, T1 or T2two points, same timepointthe difference vector, its three components and its length
Distance between 2 points, T1 T2one point at T1, one at T2the same, but crossed between the two point sets: this is the asymmetry measurement
Distance point line, T1 or T2a point and a linethe component of the point orthogonal to the line
Distance point line, T1 T2two measurementsthe T2 components minus the T1 ones
Angle between 2 lines, T1 or T2two lines, same timepointan angle per anatomical plane
Angle between 2 lines, T1 T2two T1 lines, two T2 linesthe differences of the angles
Angle line T1 and line T2a line at each timepointthe same angle computation across timepoints

Each yaw, pitch and roll is the angle in the axial, sagittal and coronal plane respectively. The measurement code returns absolute values with labels (right or left, anterior or posterior, superior or inferior; mesial or distal for teeth; clockwise or counter-clockwise for angles between timepoints), and the post-processing gives them back a sign from the label. Two opposite conventions coexist for the vertical axis, skeletal and dental (details).

In the codeThe exact type strings, the formulas with their lines and the sign-meaning tables

5.2 The default list

The default measurement lists are downloaded separately; the file name decides the role of each (cranial base, mandible, maxilla, feature template).

FileContents
Cranial base list26 distances and 4 angles
Mandible list15 distances (a subset of the first)
Maxilla list7 distances (a subset of the first)
Feature templatethe header: an ID, 14 features, then the three answers

The cranial-base list holds 16 distances between T1 and T2, which is the direct asymmetry. The pattern: a midline point is compared to itself in the mirror (A, B, Me), a lateral point to its counterpart on the other side (right gonion against left gonion) since the mirror has swapped sides. The 10 T1-only distances are classic anatomical lengths. The 4 angles are two gonial angles (T1 only, sharing a vertex, hence complemented to 180 degrees) and two angles between a T1 line and a T2 line; reversing the order of the points on the T2 side is deliberate, because the mirror swapped left and right and the two vectors then become almost collinear, so the angle tends toward 0 for a symmetric patient.

In the codeThe lists with their landmark pairs and the first-sheet-only reading

5.3 Deriving the maxillary landmarks without a second ALI pass

ALI is only run once, in the cranial-base frame. The landmarks in the maxillary frame are obtained through a rigid composition.

Both orientation transforms share the same centred scan as their reference, so centring cancels out and the composition is exact. The gain is a few minutes per patient and a single manual editing point: correcting a landmark at the review pause automatically propagates to the maxillary frame.

In the codeThe composition code, the transform files it needs and how ALI’s group labels are read

5.4 From measurement to feature

The measurement step produces one row per patient and measurement with 16 columns. A first function flattens this into signed columns (transverse, anterior-posterior, vertical, 3D, yaw, pitch, roll, plus tooth columns), dropping columns that are entirely empty, so the mandible and maxilla tables have no angular columns. The post-processing then goes the other way: it decodes each column name of the feature template to find which row of which table to read. A name tells the source table, the landmarks and the component; when it names more than two landmark segments, the feature is an average of the components of the corresponding lines. The output has one row per patient and empty answer columns, which the classifier fills.

In the codeThe column-name grammar with its example and the output file

6. The classifier

Three joblib files are loaded. They are LightGBM classifier models, not a network and not a hand-written rule tree. Hyperparameters and tree counts were confirmed by unpickling the files.

ModelFeaturesTrees kept
Symmetry14459
Mandible involved73
Maxilla involved711

The 5,000 estimators announced are never reached (early stopping during training), so the sub-models are tiny. The main model rests for about 90 % of its gain on two measurements: the transverse deviation of the chin (the menton against its mirror) and the vertical difference between the two ramus branches.

Chaining the three models. The symmetry model answers every patient: asymmetric (class 0) or symmetric (class 1). The mandible and maxilla answers start as “False”, and only the asymmetric cases go through the two sub-models. They are independent: both can be true. The output is the feature table with the three answer columns; this is the final deliverable.

In the codeThe hyperparameters, the 14 features with their gain, and the chaining code

7. Manual review and batch navigation

7.1 The pauses

A step of the plan can ask the panel to stop at its end, load the results into the scene and wait. Three kinds of pause exist: view only (loaded and shown, nothing rewritten); editable landmarks (unlocked and shown, and rewritten only if a point moved); and adjustable registration (the registered scan overlays the original at half opacity with interaction handles, and the correction is composed into the registration’s own transform, because AutoMatrix downstream can only apply a single matrix). The quantification block is placed before the visualisation block, so that the one really useful editable pause does not arrive after five nnU-Net segmentations.

In the codeThe catalogue of pauses with their availability, and the Qt event-loop constraint

7.2 Batch navigation: what exists

The port of the review from AREG has been done. The panel offers previous and next patient (saving the current edit first), the position in the batch, flagging, and a selective rollback that walks back to the nearest correctable step and replays the steps in between for the flagged patients only. In VISOR the pause-and-review system is not ported.

In the codeThe functions of the navigation and the rollback logic

7.3 Batch navigation: what is genuinely missing

Only some steps can be narrowed to the flagged patients. For the others, a rollback replays the whole batch: for example both AMASSS runs, the Elastix registration of the maxilla and the mandible, or all five bone-surface runs. Narrowing the measurement and classification steps would be harmful, since they write an aggregated table rebuilt from scratch; the proper fix is to add the scan keys, not every folder key (details).

In the codeThe list of narrowable input keys and the two lesser limits

Folder under the output folderContentsKept after cleanup

Masks, mirror, registered scansAMASSS masks, the second scan, the registered scans and matricesno
Landmarks (T1, mirrored, mirrored and registered)the landmark files at each stageno

Classificationthe final tableyes if Quantitative
Bone surfaces and HeatmapsBDS surfaces and one signed-distance file per patient and zoneyes if Visualization

The maps are computed over three zones (merged cranial base, mandible, upper skull). Each pair runs in its own process because the VTK filter allocates memory that the garbage collector does not free; beyond a million points in total the surfaces are thinned by half, beyond two million by 70 %, and the distance is carried back onto the original mesh. Unless “Keep intermediate files” is checked, every other sub-folder is deleted at the end; a past bug there could wipe the wrong folder and is fixed (details).

10. Traps and fragile spots

Each point is checked in the code and described, with its evidence, on the code page.

  • The maxillary landmark derivation fails outside “Full pipeline”. The transform files are not copied, so the maxilla table is empty and five features out of fourteen are missing. Evidence.
  • CMFReg does not change the registration. Evidence.
  • Hardcoded T1 and T2 suffixes. A third timepoint silently breaks the pairing. Evidence.
  • Keyword-based mask selection. The cranial-base mask beats the mandible mask only by alphabetical order. Evidence.
  • A repeated landmark label keeps only its first row. Evidence.
  • The Python steps freeze the interface. Evidence.
  • Only the first sheet of a measurement list is read. Evidence.

11. Literature

The corresponding published work is “Automated classification of skeletal facial asymmetry in CBCT using a reproducible 3D Slicer workflow for patient-specific decision support”, SPIE Medical Imaging 2026, vol. 13929, art. 139292J, doi:10.1117/12.3087010 (SPIE page). The public abstract describes 170 Class III patients and the same upstream chain: standardised preprocessing, mirroring, voxel registration, extraction of anatomical measurements.

The code diverges from the paper at the last step. The abstract describes an unsupervised approach (dimensionality reduction, then spectral clustering); the module ships three supervised binary LightGBM classifiers trained on 14 fixed features, and nothing in the repository links their training labels to the paper’s clusters.
