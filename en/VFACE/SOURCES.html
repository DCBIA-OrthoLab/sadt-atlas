VFACE — sources

Bibliography for VFACE: what is published, what is left to check online, and the neighboring work that sheds light on the problem without describing this code.

← VFACE page

VFACE is partially published, and what is published does not describe what the module actually runs. The SPIE Medical Imaging 2026 proceedings paper (doi:10.1117/12.3087010) describes the same upstream chain — standardized preprocessing, mirroring, voxel registration, extraction of anatomical measurements — but stops at an unsupervised clustering step (dimensionality reduction PCA / t-SNE / UMAP, then Spectral Clustering with kNN or RBF affinity, UMAP+RBF giving the best silhouette score). The module, on the other hand, ships three supervised binary LightGBM classifiers trained on 14 fixed features. These are not the same model: nothing in the repository links the LightGBMs' training labels to the paper's clusters, and the training script is not versioned.

Trap

Do not cite the paper's numbers as the module's performance.

The module's clinical principle — registering the patient onto their own reflection and measuring the gap — is not new: it comes from a series of works by the same group, 2011-2012, listed under "Neighboring work." None of these works describes this code.

Neighboring work

These works do not describe VFACE's code. They shed light on the problem.

  • The mirror method, within this same group. AlHadidi 2011 (DMFR) directly compares the two strategies: mirroring across a mid-sagittal plane defined by landmarks, versus mirroring across an arbitrary plane followed by a voxel registration on the cranial base. Cevidanes 2011 (OOOOE) validates both on simulated data. AlHadidi 2012 adds SPHARM-PDM, Paniagua 2011 adds tensor-based morphometry. It is the second strategy — mirror then voxel registration — that VFACE implements (AutoMatrix applies Matrix_mirror.tfm, AREG-CBCT then registers on CB/MAX/MAND). None of these papers mentions a classifier: they produce distance maps, not labels.
  • The measurement frame. Ruellas 2016 (AJODO) establishes the common coordinate system (Frankfort / mid-sagittal / transporionic) and the AP-SI-RL sign conventions that the measurements VFACE extracts through AQ3DC depend on.
  • The automated building blocks. Gillot 2023 describes ALI-CBCT (landmark identification), Anchling 2023 describes ASO + AREG-CBCT (orientation and registration), Bianchi 2020 describes the full set of 3D Slicer craniomaxillofacial modules. VFACE calls them; it is not their subject.
  • The state of the art outside the group. Peng 2026 (European Journal of Orthodontics) is a systematic review of automated 3D facial asymmetry assessment. It places mirror-based approaches among the others; it does not cover VFACE, published after it.
  • The mirroring method, applied clinically. The same group published four studies that use mirroring and registration without automating or classifying them: mandibular asymmetry in Class I with unilateral crossbite (Evangelista 2020, AJODO), craniofacial asymmetry in children after rapid maxillary expansion (Evangelista 2020, Orthod Craniofac Res), prevalence of mandibular asymmetry across sagittal patterns (Evangelista 2022, Angle Orthod, open access), and mandibular canal displacement in ameloblastoma (method described in 2011). None of them describes an automated pipeline or a learned model.
  • The same idea, outside the group. España-Pamplona 2023 (Head & Face Medicine, open access) quantifies mandibular asymmetry through segmentation then mirroring in 3D Slicer, with classes close to what VFACE returns. Independent Spanish team: no connection to this code.
  • AQ3DC, the measuring brick. The generic tool that computes distances and angles between points, reused upstream of VFACE's measurements, is documented by its NA-MIC pages — PW38 (Gran Canaria) and PW39 (Montreal) — and by the Q3DCExtension repository. Neither mentions asymmetry, mirroring or classification: AQ3DC measures, it does not decide.

Addendum: three upstream texts retrieved afterward

NIH author manuscripts return an empty fullTextXML at Europe PMC, which is what had them classified as "to check". The efetch route serves them:

curl -sL "https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=<number without PMC>"
