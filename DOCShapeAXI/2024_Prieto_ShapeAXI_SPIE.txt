## Abstract
ShapeAXI represents a cutting-edge framework for shape analysis that leverages a multi-view approach, capturing 3D objects from diverse viewpoints and subsequently analyzing them via 2D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). We implement an automatic N-fold cross-validation process and aggregate the results across all folds. This ensures insightful explainability heat-maps for each class across every shape, enhancing interpretability and contributing to a more nuanced understanding of the underlying phenomena.

We demonstrate the versatility of ShapeAXI through two targeted classification experiments. The first experiment categorizes condyles into healthy and degenerative states. The second, more intricate experiment, engages with shapes extracted from CBCT scans of cleft patients, efficiently classifying them into four severity classes. This innovative application not only aligns with existing medical research but also opens new avenues for specialized cleft patient analysis, holding considerable promise for both scientific exploration and clinical practice.

The rich insights derived from ShapeAXI’s explainability images reinforce existing knowledge and provide a platform for fresh discovery in the fields of condyle assessment and cleft patient severity classification. As a versatile and interpretative tool, ShapeAXI sets a new benchmark in 3D object interpretation and classification, and its groundbreaking approach hopes to make significant contributions to research and practical applications across various domains.

ShapeAXI is available in our GitHub repository https://github.com/DCBIA-OrthoLab/ShapeAXI


## INTRODUCTION
ShapeAXI represents an innovative approach in the field of shape analysis, employing a multi-view approach that captures 3D objects from various viewpoints. This approach is strategically designed to analyze the objects through specially tailored 2D Neural Networks (NN), ensuring a comprehensive assessment.

We present two distinct classification experiments that demonstrate the framework’s capabilities and adaptability. The first experiment analyzes condyles, classifying them into categories of healthy and degenerative states. The second experiment analyzes shapes derived from CBCT scans of cleft patients, classifying these into four severity classes. ShapeAXI performs an automatic N-fold cross-validation process across both experiments, runs testing, and creates explainability heat maps for each class across every shape. These visual representations provide unprecedented insights into the underlying object characteristics and classification logic. This feature not only aligns with existing knowledge but also paves the way for new explorations.

The introduction of ShapeAXI marks a contribution for 3D object interpretation and classification. By coupling complex shape analysis with intuitive explainability and interpretability, ShapeAXI stands as a groundbreaking tool that could influence research, medical practice, and analytical processes across various fields. Its application to both condyles and cleft patient shapes showcases its versatility and potential, enriching our understanding and opening new avenues for specialized analysis.


## RELATED WORK

### 3D shape analysis
Among the existing approaches for shape analysis, learning-based methods have shown better performance. There are primarily four categories of learning-based methods: multi-view, volumetric, parametric, and multi-layer perceptrons (MLP).

Multi-view approaches adapt existing state-of-the-art 2D CNNs to function with 3D shapes. By rendering 3D objects from diverse viewpoints, they facilitate feature extraction using 2D NNs., Volumetric methods, on the other hand, employ 3D voxel grids to represent the shape and leverage 3D convolutions for shape feature learning., Parametric approaches necessitate shapes with spherical topology, applying convolution directly to the shape’s spherical representation., Lastly, some techniques consume point clouds directly, utilizing multi-layer-perceptrons or transformer architectures.,

Our method aligns with the multi-view category, rendering the object and capturing 2D images from various viewpoints based on an icosahedron subdivision. This multi-view approach is flexible and offers the advantage of operating on surfaces of any topology, including those with missing data or holes. With our multi-view approach, we have contributed to the development of algorithms for dental model segmentation?,?

Our explainability model is agnostic to the input data and does not require systematic perturbations to produce explanations. Moreover, it does not require subsampling the input data through encoder/decoder architectures and does not require shapes with a spherical topology or specialized operators such as spherical convolutions. In our experiments, we use spheres of +160000 vertex at full resolution.


### Explainable Artificial Intelligence
With Machine Learning (ML) systems becoming progressively pervasive and often outperforming humans in specialized tasks, concerns related to the deployment of these complex systems are growing, particularly in domains with direct human impact such as medical imaging. Such systems must be transparent in their decision-making processes, providing explanations in terms comprehensible to non-experts. Explainability methods can be broadly categorized into three classes: visualization, model distillation, and intrinsic. AI systems are increasingly being used in various fields, such as adaptive email filters, car automation, predictive policing systems, and autonomous diagnostics in healthcare. These systems often outperform humans in specific tasks, but there is a growing concern about the deployment of such complex applications that have a direct impact on human lives. While most applications are optimized for task performance, safety is also crucial, and this cannot be completely quantified. To address this issue, the criterion of explainability is becoming popular. By using techniques such as Grad-CAM and RISE, we can produce explainability maps that show which features of the input are important for the model’s decision-making, and this can be mapped on the 3D model. This will help us understand the model’s reasoning and verify its correctness. Our approach to explainability is distinctive in that it is agnostic to the input data and free from the necessity of systematic perturbations or subsampling through encoder/decoder architectures. Moreover, it avoids the requirement for shapes with spherical topology or specialized operators such as spherical convolutions.


## MATERIALS
The study harnessed two distinct datasets for a multifaceted analysis. The first dataset consisted of 90 samples related to condyles, categorized into healthy subjects and those with Degenerative Joint Disease (DJD). The images were collected at the University of Pacific with an i-CAT® device, configured with 120 Kvp, 3–8 mA, 0.4-mm voxel size, and a field of view (FOV) of 23 cm × 17 cm.

The second dataset featured 190 de-identified cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) scans of patients with cleft lip and palate. These scans were acquired across three university centers and analyzed through a specific process. First, all scans were oriented using the Frankfurt horizontal plane, followed by automatic segmentations of the maxilla using the automatic multi-anatomical skull structure segmentation (AMASSS) algorithm on Slicer Automated Dental Tools. The 3D analysis was executed using two open-source software packages: ITK-SNAP, version 3.8, and 3D Slicer, version 5.1.0. This approach assessed the shape, height, and width of the alveolar bone defect, leading to a severity classification ranging from 0 to 3. Inter and intra-examiner reproducibility was thoroughly verified.

Collectively, these datasets allowed for a comprehensive examination of both condyles and cleft conditions. The usage of specific imaging parameters, along with sophisticated software for segmentation and analysis, contributed to robust insights into these distinct medical subjects.


## IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
shows the general framework of ShapeAXI. ShapeAXI automatically handles splitting the data into N folds with optional grouping criteria for the split. The data is preprocessed by computing the maximum scale across the entire dataset. All shapes were scaled by this value to standardize the data. Furthermore, the shapes were centered in the unit sphere for analysis. During training, random rotations were applied. This augmentation enhanced the model’s ability to generalize across various orientations. The NN architecture is composed by a feature extraction that is customizable with architectures available in the torchvision library and monai. For all experiments, ResNet-18 was utilized for this purpose. An additive attention layer that pools data from all the views of the object and finally a prediction layer outputs the number of classes in the data. We used the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10−4. The Early Stopping Criteria is used to avoid overfitting and automatically stop the training. We used the multi-class cross-entropy loss.


## RESULTS
The classification report for the condyle dataset shows a robust model with an accuracy of 79.78%. The healthy condyles have higher precision and recall (78.62%, 87.50%) compared to degenerative states (81.66%, 70.05%). The AUC of 0.8369 indicates a good predictive model with room for improvement in recall for degenerative states.

For the cleft dataset, the results reflect solid performance across all severity classes (0 to 3). Class 0 showcases the highest precision (0.9138), and Class 2 highlights sensitivity with a recall of 0.8444. Class 3 indicates potential misclassification with lower recall (0.7895). The overall accuracy of 0.8158 and high AUC values confirm reliable and balanced classification, demonstrating that the model is well-suited for classifying cleft severity from CBCT scans, with promising potential for clinical applications.

## CONCLUSIONS
This work introduces ShapeAXI a novel framework for shape analysis, explainability, and interpretability utilizing a multi-view approach with 2D NN to classify 3D objects. We demonstrate the approach with 2 experiments on condyles and cleft patients achieving accuracies of 79.78% and 81.58%, respectively, showing robustness and flexibility, the cleft data is challenging as it contains holes. A key feature of ShapeAXI is its distinctive explainability, providing heat maps without needing specialized methods, contributing to transparency, and increasing confidence in the decision-making process by the NN. Its application in classifying cleft severity aligns with existing research and opens new possibilities. While setting a benchmark in 3D interpretation and classification, there’s room for enhancement in sensitivity, applicability, and explainability. ShapeAXI’s innovative combination of analysis with intuitive explainability positions it as a pioneering tool with the potential to influence future developments. Future work will focus on adding functionality for regression tasks and a longitudinal analysis component.
